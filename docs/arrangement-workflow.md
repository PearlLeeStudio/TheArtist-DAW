# Arrange a score or sketch with TheArtist

TheArtist is an AI arrangement tool for developing musical material into an
editable harmonic score and accompaniment. Start with a score, enter chords,
or create a song form. Compare changes in context, choose how the parts play,
and continue the arrangement in a DAW.

## 1. Bring in the material

Use **Import Score** for a PDF or image, the **MIDI** input for a played chord,
or **Set manually** to enter harmony in a selected slot. **New from Song Form**
can create the starting material when there is no score yet.

For Import, choose the file, set **Use Meta model** if wanted, then click
**Analyze and import**. A supported vector PDF opens on the staff from the local
reader in a second or two either way. With the checkbox on, Meta then reads the
original in the background: a **Meta review** band above the staff shows its
progress and outcome, bars where the two readings disagree get a **Review** mark
with the reason, bars the local reader left empty and you have not touched take
Meta's proposal (one undo step), and chords you can already see are never
replaced. Scores over 128 bars skip the Meta reading; images and PDFs the local
reader cannot read are read by Meta directly. Bars that stayed unresolved carry
an **Unresolved** mark, and key, meter, chords and section boundaries are
corrected on the staff. Only unusable project fields (key, meter, tempo, section
ranges) stop in the dialog for correction before **Import reviewed score**.

The imported result is harmonic material. Note evidence can support chord
interpretation, but melody notes and durations are not imported as a melody
track. Keep the source available for melodic and instrumental work in the DAW.
See [score analysis](score-analysis.md) and [MIDI input](midi-input.md).

## 2. Shape harmony in context

Select the relevant slots on the staff and open **Input**.

| Tool | Use it for | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| Generate | New harmonic material for a selected passage | A proposed progression for the selected slots |
| Reharmonize | Alternatives to chords already in the score | A bounded set of substitutions within the selected slots |
| Set manually / MIDI | A specific harmonic decision | The chosen slot |

Select slots by clicking or dragging across the staff; Ctrl/⌘+A selects the whole
score. Ctrl/⌘+X, C and V cut, copy and paste the selected slots (right-click offers
the same): a copied passage that divides the selection exactly repeats over it,
so a two-bar pattern fills eight selected bars; anything else is written forward
from the first selected slot and appends bars as needed. Delete clears the
selection and Ctrl/⌘+Z undoes cut, paste and clear. The clipboard carries the
chords with their voicings and the text form `Dm7 G7 | Cmaj7 | -`, which can
also be pasted from any text source.

In **Reharmonize**, choose **Change up to 1, 2, 3 or 4 chords** and a mode.
This sets the maximum per candidate, not a required number of edits or the
number of alternatives. The result always offers three candidates when the
search succeeds. It may favor fewer changes when they fit the passage.
Select existing chords across at most eight measures and sixteen occupied
slots; empty slots stay unchanged. The surrounding harmony informs the search.

In Reharmonize, the **Origin / Alter 1 / Alter 2 / Alter 3** cards share one row.
Select alternatives together, then use **Play** to audition the result. A new
overlapping choice replaces the conflicting card. **Apply** keeps the selection and
closes the cards (Ctrl/⌘+Z undoes it); **×** discards changes since the last Apply.
**Origin** previews the original symbols and voicings. Generate retains its separate **Original/A/B/C** controls. Compare a passage
in the context of the arrangement, including how it leads into the next section.
An explicit mode constrains substitutions; Auto lets the search consider
contextual harmonic colors. The [Reharmonize guide](reharmonization.md) explains
its mathematical candidates, learned scoring and Meta selection.

## 3. Develop the form

Use section labels to navigate and edit boundaries. **New from Song Form**
can append a section or extend a sketch into a full form.

| Full-form option | Harmonic material |
|---|---|
| Muse Spark only | Meta plans the form and writes the new chords; validation precedes application |
| Music Transformer + Meta review | Meta plans and reviews sections generated by the author's model and form rules |
| Genre template | The genre form and Music Transformer work without a Meta call |

Keep the existing sketch when extending it. Choose **Start a new song** for a
fresh arrangement: the current score is saved in **Projects** first, and the
new song retains key, genre, tempo and meter. If saving fails, the existing
score stays open. Generation targets follow the available remaining length;
after Undo the dialog selects a valid target again.

For returning choruses, **Keep the theme** can reuse an earlier complete
progression with cadence adjustments; **New progression** asks for fresh
material. Review the resulting form and harmonic transitions before arranging
the accompaniment.

## 4. Arrange and audition the parts

Open **Play** to choose the harmony sound, comping rhythm and drum pattern.
Auto uses genre defaults; explicit choices override them. Piano and guitar
views show the active chord voicing. Adjust tempo and the independent Harmony,
Bass and Drums levels, plus Master, and mute parts to hear their contribution.

The available controls include 31 harmony sounds, 13 comping rhythms and
15 drum patterns. These accompaniment parts use application rules and presets.
The AI works on harmony, form and comparison; it does not independently compose
orchestrated instrumental parts. Samples render browser playback, while MIDI
keeps the arrangement editable in the receiving DAW.

## 5. Save and continue in a DAW

**Save** stores the song and playback settings in this browser's **Projects**.
Project cards can reopen the arrangement or export their own complete score and
settings. Browser storage is local; there is no account-based cloud sync.

**Export MIDI** produces separate Harmony, Bass and Drums parts with tempo,
meter, section markers and mixer information. Continue editing instrumental
parts, melody and production in the DAW. **Open in Logic Pro** from a hosted
editor uses the [local Mac bridge](midi-input.md#open-a-hosted-score-in-logic-pro);
MIDI download works without it. Ableton handoff reveals the MIDI file in Finder (local backend or the bridge).

**Score PDF** shares the harmonic score. The separate
[Logic Scripter export](../plugin/logic-scripter/README.md) supplies a MIDI FX
script for Harmony and Bass, with its own transport behavior and limitations.

## AI roles and verification

| Component | Role in the arrangement |
|---|---|
| Music Transformer and genre LoRAs | Generate chord material and score substitution candidates in context |
| Meta Standard Muse Spark | Read an attached score when requested, plan harmonic form, compare substitutions and review candidates |
| Application music engine | Validate edits, compute voicings, apply accompaniment patterns, mix playback and render MIDI |
| Musician | Review imported evidence, choose harmony and form, audition the parts and finish the arrangement |

Generation, playback and export each have separate checks. A successful model
response does not establish listening quality, and a MIDI file opening in a DAW
does not prove identical instrument sounds. The [evaluation guide](evaluation.md)
separates component measurements from [browser, audio and DAW evidence](manual-checks.md).
The current scope is harmonic arrangement with three accompaniment parts;
melody-track import, vocal generation, audio mastering and a native AU/VST3
plugin are not implemented.
